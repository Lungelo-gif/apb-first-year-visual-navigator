# APB checkpoint photography checklist

## Before your campus walk

- [ ] Confirm photography permission and any campus restrictions. Get appropriate guidance for public-facing Protection Services photos.
- [ ] Confirm the actual starting location, public library endpoint, chosen laboratory and chosen classroom.
- [ ] Plan the route in person. Identify every turn, fork, entrance and destination sign a first-year student needs to recognise.
- [ ] Check access times, temporary closures, crossings, surfaces and step-free alternatives with campus staff. Do not claim accessibility based on photos alone.
- [ ] List shared checkpoints once so routes reuse the same approved photo and node.
- [ ] Prepare a private shot log: node ID, filename, visible landmark, direction, next checkpoint, access notes and permission/review status.

## Taking photos with the iPhone 13 Pro Max

- [ ] Use ordinary landscape still photos, holding the phone level at a normal walking viewpoint. Start with the main 1× camera and move back safely if a wider view is needed.
- [ ] Face the direction a student will travel. Show enough surrounding context to recognise the location and next movement.
- [ ] Take a clear checkpoint photo before each turn, then another after a confusing turn if useful. Add more checkpoints where the real route needs them.
- [ ] Make public signs readable. Avoid blur, strong glare and shots so wide that essential details disappear on a phone.
- [ ] Capture the arrival sign or public entrance. Do not enter restricted spaces to get a better angle.
- [ ] Take a spare frame at each checkpoint. Do not photograph while crossing a road or obstructing a walkway.
- [ ] Remember: these are wide sequential still photos, not a true 360° panorama system.

## Privacy and security review — every image

- [ ] No recognisable faces without permission. Wait for a clear frame or crop safely; get a new photo if the important context would be lost.
- [ ] No student cards, names, student numbers, documents or other personal information.
- [ ] No computer screens containing information, including reflections.
- [ ] No visible vehicle registration numbers.
- [ ] No security equipment, access-control details or restricted areas.
- [ ] Protection Services photos stop at the public entrance or official sign. No security-office interior.
- [ ] No copied UJ logos, protected imagery or assets from another application. If a required official destination sign raises permission questions, confirm appropriate use before publishing it.
- [ ] Review the entire image at full resolution, including background details and reflections.

## Before adding files to the project

- [ ] Keep camera originals privately outside the public repository.
- [ ] Export to JPEG; HEIC files renamed to `.jpg` will not work.
- [ ] Aim for 1,600–2,000 pixels wide and roughly 100–250 KB each. Adjust compression while keeping signs readable; avoid multi-megabyte camera originals.
- [ ] Remove GPS/location and other unnecessary metadata from the exported files. The application does not remove EXIF metadata for you.
- [ ] Use clear lowercase filenames such as `library-02-turn-left.jpg` and `shared-01-start.jpg`.
- [ ] Write useful alternative text describing the actual view, not just “photo 1”.
- [ ] Check that the instruction, landmark and photo agree with one another.
- [ ] Review the final exported files again, then test on a phone over mobile data.
- [ ] Walk each completed route with a reviewer who did not help author it. Keep the route marked as a sample until the real route is verified.
